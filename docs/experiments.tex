\section{Introduction}
This document is the living synthesis of the experiment campaigns; the
markdown reports in \texttt{experiments/} are the primary record. Each section
transcribes (never recomputes) the quantitative results of a campaign,
preserves its run and dataset identifiers verbatim for traceability, and adds
the rationale and interpretation that the terse reports omit.

\paragraph{Task and target.} The lab predicts a per-track \emph{engagement}
score, defined as $\text{play\_count}\times\text{completion\_ratio}$, from a
track's intrinsic metadata over the user's listening corpus. Because
engagement is heavy-tailed and
non-negative, models are trained against the $\log1p$-transformed target and
predictions are mapped back with $\text{expm1}$. This compresses the long tail
into a regime where squared-error losses behave, but it carries a hazard: a
model that is unbounded in log-space can produce a large positive log
prediction that $\text{expm1}$ amplifies into an enormous engagement value (the
``$\text{expm1}$ blow-up''). Bounded-by-construction families are immune;
unbounded ones are not (see Section~\ref{sec:background}).

\paragraph{Metrics.} The headline metric is the mean absolute error (MAE) in
raw engagement units, reported alongside RMSE and the coefficient of
determination \rsq{}. MAE is robust to the tail and is the quantity the
leaderboard sorts on; RMSE exposes tail misses; \rsq{} measures variance
explained relative to the median baseline. Absolute-percentage error
(\medape{}) is used where a scale-free view helps. Throughout, MAE is in raw
engagement units with thousands separators where large.

\paragraph{Features.} The feature space is a track's \emph{intrinsic metadata}
alone: genre, artist, artist/track popularity, follower count, release year,
credited-artist count and release type. The 200-dimension co-listening
word2vec embedding is a \emph{behavioral} signal, not metadata, and is left
out of the feature set entirely --- the payload-based predictors are the
canonical models here. Critically, the recipe is \emph{leakage-free}: the
playback fields that mechanically constitute or proxy the target ---
\texttt{play\_count}, \texttt{completion\_ratio}, \texttt{skip\_rate},
\texttt{shuffle\_rate}, \texttt{distinct\_sessions} --- and the behaviorally
entangled curation fields are all excluded by default.

\section{Background: model families and the log-target}
\label{sec:background}
This section gives the minimum theory for a reader who knows regression but
not this repo. It is keyed to \texttt{PROJECT-FACTS.md} and grows only when a
new predictor family enters the reports. No campaigns have run yet; the
families below are the expected opening contenders.

\begin{description}
  \item[Gradient-boosted trees (xgboost).] An additive ensemble of shallow
  regression trees, each fit to the residual gradient of the previous stage.
  Predictions are leaf averages, so the output is bounded by the range of
  training targets: the model \emph{cannot} blow up through the $\text{expm1}$
  inverse. Trees capture non-linear interactions among PCA components without
  manual feature crosses, which suits a dense embedded feature space; the cost
  is a non-deterministic fit (subsampling and column sampling carry an internal
  RNG seed), so two runs at different seeds differ slightly.
  \item[Ridge regression.] Ordinary least squares with an $\ell_2$ penalty on
  the coefficients (here with automatic $\alpha$ selection). It is a linear
  model, so it is fully deterministic given the data --- the closed-form
  solution has no RNG --- and run-to-run variance is exactly zero. Being
  linear and unbounded in log-space, it is in principle capable of an
  $\text{expm1}$ blow-up, but with the $\ell_2$ penalty and this corpus it
  reverts toward a sane intercept and stays bounded in practice. It serves as a
  strong, cheap, reproducible baseline and as a control on the training
  harness itself.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Why metadata-only and the log-target.} Restricting features to
intrinsic metadata makes the model a cold-start scorer: it predicts
engagement for a track with zero plays, isolating how much taste fit is
explainable from catalog attributes versus behavioral co-listening. The
$\log1p$ target tames the engagement tail at train time, and $\text{expm1}$
restores raw units at predict time --- the transform pair is the reason the
blow-up hazard exists and the reason bounded families are preferred until an
unbounded one is proven safe.
